import os
import sys
import time
from datetime import datetime

from google import genai

API_KEY = os.getenv("GEMINI_API_KEY")
MODEL = "gemini-3.8-flash"
#PROMPT = "Whether smooth, reasonable starting conditions in a three-demensional
 incompressible fluid will always remain smooth for all time, or if they can blo
w up."
PROMPT = "Whether every 'hole-free' three-dimensional shape (a three-sphere) is
essentially a sphere."
RETRY_INTERVAL = 60  # seconds

def main():

    if not API_KEY:
        print("ERROR: GEMINI_API_KEY environment variable not found.")
        sys.exit(1)

    client = genai.Client(api_key=API_KEY)

    attempt = 0

    print("=" * 60)
    print("Google Gemini API - Continuous Connection Test")
    print(f"Model: {MODEL}")
    print(f"Retry interval: {RETRY_INTERVAL} seconds")
    print("=" * 60)

    while True:

        attempt += 1

        print(
            f"\n[{datetime.now():%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S}] "
            f"Attempt #{attempt}: Connecting to Google..."
        )

        try:

            response = client.models.generate_content(
                model=MODEL,
                contents=PROMPT,
            )

            result = response.text

            if result and result.strip():

                print("\n" + "=" * 60)
                print("SUCCESS - VALID RESPONSE RECEIVED!")
                print("=" * 60)
                print(result)

                sys.exit(0)

            else:

                print("Google returned an empty response.")

        except KeyboardInterrupt:

            print("\nStopped by user.")
            sys.exit(1)

        except Exception as exc:

            print(f"Connection failed: {type(exc).__name__}: {exc}")

        # Retry after one minute
        print(f"\nNext attempt in {RETRY_INTERVAL} seconds...")
        time.sleep(RETRY_INTERVAL)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

The statement you are describing is one of the most famous problems in the histo

it was finally proven by Russian mathematician **Grigori Perelman** in 2002-2003
.

Here is a breakdown of what that statement actually means, why it was so difficu
lt, and how it was resolved:

---

### 1. Translating the Terminology
To understand the conjecture, it helps to translate the everyday words into the
language of **topology** (the branch of math that studies shapes based on their
fundamental connectivity, ignoring rigid measurements like angles or lengths):

* **"Essentially a sphere":** In topology, two shapes are considered "essentiall
y the same" (homeomorphic) if you can deform one into the other by stretching, b
ending, and squishing, but **without cutting, tearing, or gluing**. A deflated b
asketball, a cubical balloon, and a smooth marble are all "essentially spheres."

* **"Three-dimensional shape" (3-manifold):** An ordinary sphere (like the surfa
ce of the Earth) is actually **two-dimensional**-you only need two coordinates (
latitude and longitude) to describe any location on it. A **3-sphere** is the hi
gher-dimensional counterpart: it is a 3D "surface" that bounds a 4-dimensional b
all. If you lived inside a 3-sphere, you could travel in any 3D direction (up/do
wn, left/right, forward/backward) and eventually end up back where you started,
without ever hitting a wall.
* **"Hole-free" (Simply connected):** Imagine taking a giant rubber band (a lass
o) and throwing it anywhere in the space. If the space is "simply connected," yo
u can always pull that lasso completely tight down to a single point without it
getting snagged on a hole (like a donut hole) or an obstacle.

### 2. The Question Poincaré Asked
Poincaré knew that for a **2D surface**, being finite, having no edges, and havi
ng no holes meant it **had** to be a standard 2-sphere.

He asked: **Does the same rule apply in three dimensions?**
> *If a 3D space is finite, has no boundaries, and is completely free of holes (
simply connected), is it guaranteed to be just a distorted 3-sphere?*

For decades, mathematicians could not prove this. Counterintuitively, the conjec
ture was proven true for higher dimensions long before it was solved for three:
* In 1961, Stephen Smale proved it was true for **dimension 5 and higher**.
* In 1982, Michael Freedman proved it was true for **dimension 4**.
* Yet dimension 3 resisted all attempts. In 3D, there is "just enough room" for
shapes to become unimaginably tangled, but not enough room to easily untangle th
em as one can in higher dimensions.

### 3. The Resolution
In 2000, the Clay Mathematics Institute designated the Poincaré Conjecture as on
e of the **seven Millennium Prize Problems**, offering a $1 million reward for i
ts solution.

Between 2002 and 2003, **Grigori Perelman** posted a series of papers online tha
t provided the proof. He used a technique known as **Ricci flow with surgery** (
pioneered by mathematician Richard Hamilton).
* Think of Ricci flow like a heat equation for geometry: it smooths out the lump
s, bumps, and bottlenecks of a shape over time.
* Perelman showed that any hole-free 3D shape, when smoothed out this way, will
inevitably round out into a perfect 3-sphere, proving Poincaré right.

### 4. The Aftermath
Perelman's proof was thoroughly verified by the global mathematical community by
 2006. In recognition of his achievement, he was offered:
1. The **Fields Medal** (often called the "Nobel Prize of Mathematics") in 2006.

2. The **$1,000,000 Millennium Prize** in 2010.

Famous for his reclusiveness and indifference to fame and money, Perelman **decl
ined both awards**, stating that his contribution was no greater than Richard Ha
milton's, who had laid the groundwork.

To this day, the Poincaré Conjecture is the **only one** of the seven Millennium
 Prize Problems to have been solved.
